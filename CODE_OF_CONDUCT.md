# Code of Conduct at Launch, Humanity's Charter

The Charter is a framework for what comes next, open to everyone, shaped through debate, built together. These are the founder's temporary participation rules for working on the Charter. The founder remains accountable for their application and may appoint moderators with clearly stated responsibilities. Their authority covers conduct in the project's spaces. Binding Charter amendments and future public governance require their own agreed processes.

These rules apply to discussion in the temporary Discord Governance Incubator and to ideas sent through the website's private review form. Other official project channels must state when they adopt these rules. GitHub stores Charter documents and their change history; Charter debate happens in the Governance Incubator. Anyone can challenge the Charter or bring a counter-proposal.

---

## 1. Argue the idea, never the person

Attack proposals, not people. Challenge the weakest position in the room, and offer a better one. A counter-proposal is stronger than a dismissal.

Registers differ. Direct challenge sounds different from consensus-style objection; both are the same work. Try: "That assumption is weak, here is a replacement" (direct), or "I think this misses the context, let me restate it from my ground" (consensus), or "I cannot speak for my region, but from my own experience this does not hold" (high-context). None of these attack the person; all of them argue the idea.

## 2. One voice, many registers

Different people argue differently. "Challenge" and "attack" are both welcome; contempt is not. If a word lands harshly in your region, say so plainly, and restate it in your own register. Precision and care are part of the argument.

## 3. Do not speak for everyone

The Charter is global from day one. Never claim to speak for a whole region, discipline, or culture. Speak from your own ground, and name your ground. "Everyone" is presumptuous; "much of the world" is honest.

## 4. Say when you are wrong, if you can

This project runs on honesty. When a counter-proposal beats yours, saying so strengthens the argument. But public self-correction is a cultural register, not a duty; in some places it is expected, in others it is not how people speak. The record shows who was answered either way. Honesty is the working agreement; the form of admitting is yours.

## 5. Keep the process visible

Changes to the Charter's public text must be visible and explained. Governance concerns, including possible capture or process drift, can be raised with the founder or an appointed moderator. Concerns involving people should be reported privately.

The proposed lifecycle with labels and visible change states, audit functions, and an ethics function remain ideas for future governance. The proposed ethics function would watch for capture, abuse, and process drift and report its findings without voting. These functions are not established launch institutions.

## 6. Keep governance proposals open

The foundation, the proposed categories for principles, proposals, and open questions, and the aim of equal participation remain open for debate. The seat-and-vote guarantee is a proposal for future governance; voting eligibility and procedures are undecided. No community has ratified a protected core or binding voting process. Any future adopted principles would change through that community's agreed process.

## 7. Care for the least-voiced

The Charter must work for everyone. Watch for whose voice is missing, and make room for it. Power asymmetry is real; the process is the guard against it.

## Reports and moderation

Report a conduct concern privately to the founder using the contact details published on the site, or by private message to an appointed moderator. Explain what happened and provide relevant context. A conduct report is separate from the website's proposal form, which is for ideas about the Charter. Access to case details is limited to the people needed to handle the report and any reconsideration.

Threats, harassment, personal abuse, and sharing someone else's private information breach these rules. Disagreement with the Charter, the founder, or a moderator is welcome. A moderation decision must identify the conduct that breached a rule.

Responses should be proportionate to the conduct and its effect on others. They may include a warning, removal of offending content, or a temporary restriction on participation. Severe or repeated breaches may lead to a longer or permanent restriction. Immediate protective action may be taken while a serious concern is reviewed.

A person affected by a moderation action receives an explanation of the rule involved and the action taken. They can provide context and request reconsideration privately. The explanation must protect other people's private information. Where an uninvolved appointed moderator is available, they should handle reconsideration. If no such moderator is available, the founder reviews the request and explains who is handling it and any conflict of interest.

Case records stay private. Any public summary must omit identifying and sensitive details, including details that could identify someone indirectly. Errors in the record can be corrected. Changes to these participation rules must be published and explained.
